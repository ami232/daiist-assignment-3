Based on Ford’s FY2025 Form 10-K, the structural market and regulatory factors that could slow down Ford's electric vehicle (EV) business in the U.S. include:

* **Lower-than-anticipated industrywide EV adoption rates:** Driven by changes in consumer sentiment, competitive dynamics, legal and policy changes, and shifts in consumer preference [ford_10k_fy2025, Item 7, p. 43-44; Item 1A, p. 26].
* **Reduction or elimination of U.S. policy incentives:** Recent policy changes in the United States have reduced or eliminated supply- and demand-side EV incentives, alongside a regulatory framework shifting away from supporting electrified vehicles [ford_10k_fy2025, Item 7, p. 43-44; Item 1A, p. 26].
* **Changes in federal emissions and fuel economy standards:** Potentially significant reductions in the stringency of federal emissions and fuel economy standards, combined with federal legislation eliminating the authority of California and other states to implement and enforce their own stringent emissions standards and zero-emission vehicle sales requirements, have added to the disruption of the U.S. EV market [ford_10k_fy2025, Item 7, p. 43-44].
* **Vehicle pricing dynamics and high battery costs:** Near-term pricing pressures and intense competitive pricing actions affect the EV market [ford_10k_fy2025, Item 7, p. 43-44; Item 1A, p. 27]. Battery costs also remain high, which hinders EVs from reaching pricing parity with internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles [ford_10k_fy2025, Item 1A, p. 27].
* **Capacity underutilization:** Slower market adoption can lead to an extended period of underutilization of EV and battery production capacity across the industry, risking the recovery of significant investments [ford_10k_fy2025, Item 7, p. 43-44; Item 1A, p. 27].
