## q01 (single)
Q: What is the deadline for a controller to notify a personal data breach to the supervisory authority?
REF: Without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after becoming aware of it, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals. If it takes longer than 72 hours, the reasons for the delay must be given.
EXPECTED: GDPR | Article 33
ANSWER: The controller must notify the personal data breach to the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it, unless the personal data breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons [GDPR, Article 33]. 

If the notification is not made within 72 hours, it must be accompanied by reasons for the delay [GDPR, Article 33].

## q05 (single)
Q: What is the maximum administrative fine for the most serious infringements of the GDPR?
REF: Up to 20 million euros or up to 4% of the total worldwide annual turnover of the previous financial year, whichever is higher.
EXPECTED: GDPR | Article 83
ANSWER: For the most serious infringements of the GDPR (such as violations of the basic principles for processing, conditions for consent, data subjects' rights, or international data transfers), administrative fines can reach up to **20,000,000 EUR**, or in the case of an undertaking, up to **4% of the total worldwide annual turnover** of the preceding financial year, whichever is higher [GDPR, Article 83(5)].

Non-compliance with an order by the supervisory authority is subject to the same maximum fine limit [GDPR, Article 83(6)].

## q08 (single)
Q: What does the storage limitation principle mean?
REF: Personal data must be kept in a form that allows identification of data subjects for no longer than necessary for the purposes of the processing (longer only for archiving, research or statistics with safeguards).
EXPECTED: GDPR | Article 5
ANSWER: Under the storage limitation principle, personal data must be kept in a form that permits the identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed [GDPR, Article 5]. 

However, personal data may be stored for longer periods insofar as the data will be processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical purposes, provided that appropriate technical and organisational measures are implemented to safeguard the rights and freedoms of the data subject [GDPR, Article 5].

## q13 (single)
Q: Can a processor hire another processor without the controller approving it?
REF: No. The processor needs the prior specific or general written authorisation of the controller. With a general authorisation it must inform the controller of changes and give it the chance to object.
EXPECTED: GDPR | Article 28
ANSWER: No, a processor cannot hire another processor without the controller's authorisation [GDPR, Article 28]. 

The GDPR states that a processor shall not engage another processor without the prior specific or general written authorisation of the controller [GDPR, Article 28]. In the case of a general written authorisation, the processor must inform the controller of any intended changes concerning the addition or replacement of other processors, thereby giving the controller the opportunity to object to such changes [GDPR, Article 28].

## q14 (single)
Q: How long does a controller have to answer a data subject request?
REF: Without undue delay and in any case within one month of receiving the request. The period can be extended by two further months when necessary, and the data subject must be informed within the first month.
EXPECTED: GDPR | Article 12
ANSWER: The controller must provide information on the action taken on a data subject request (under Articles 15 to 22) without undue delay and at the latest within **one month** of receipt of the request [GDPR, Article 12].

This period may be extended by **two further months** where necessary, taking into account the complexity and number of the requests. If an extension is needed, the controller must inform the data subject within one month of receipt of the request, together with the reasons for the delay [GDPR, Article 12].

## q15 (single)
Q: How does the GDPR define personal data?
REF: Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (the data subject).
EXPECTED: GDPR | Article 4
ANSWER: The GDPR defines ‘personal data’ as any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person (‘data subject’) [GDPR, Article 4]. 

An identifiable natural person is someone who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to:
* an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, or an online identifier, or
* one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person [GDPR, Article 4].

REF: Yes, at any time and free of charge. After the objection the data may no longer be processed for direct marketing.
