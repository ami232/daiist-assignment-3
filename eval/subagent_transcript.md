  **Short answer:** The tree removal can in principle be billed as garden
  maintenance costs (Gartenpflege). The search results don't settle the
  playground sand. It only becomes billable if the lease provides for passing
  the cost on (Umlagevereinbarung).

  Four searches were run: tree felling, playground sand, a second try for the
  wording of § 2 Nr. 10 BetrKV, and the "recurring" requirement together with
  the lease agreement. None of them returned the text of § 2 Nr. 10 BetrKV
  itself, so I can only quote it as the BGH reproduces it.

  **1. Removing the rotten tree (Baumfällung): billable in principle**
  - The BGH held that the costs of felling a rotten tree that is no longer
  stable (morsch, nicht mehr standsicher) are, in principle, billable garden
  maintenance costs (Gartenpflege) under § 2 Nr. 10 BetrKV (BGH VIII ZR 107/20,
  Leitsatz, decided 2021-11-10).
  - **Why it counts as garden maintenance:** § 2 Nr. 10 BetrKV covers the care
  of landscaped areas "including the renewal of plants and woody plants"
  (einschließlich der Erneuerung von Pflanzen und Gehölzen). A tree counts as a
  woody plant (Gehölz). Garden maintenance (Gartenpflege) covers every measure
  that keeps the garden as a whole in good order. That includes removing plants
  that are diseased, dead or rotten and unstable. Renewal also usually requires
  removal first (BGH VIII ZR 107/20, Rn. 19–22).
  - **Not repair costs (Instandhaltungs- und Instandsetzungskosten, § 1 Abs. 2
  Nr. 2 BetrKV):** a rotten tree is not automatically a defect in the rented
  property (Mangel der Mietsache). A garden is made of many plants, and no
  particular mix of plants is owed (BGH VIII ZR 107/20, Rn. 23–25).
  - **Safety duty doesn't change this:** that the felling also meets the
  landlord's duty to keep the property safe (Verkehrssicherungspflicht) does not
  make it a repair cost (BGH VIII ZR 107/20, Rn. 23–25).
  - **No replanting needed:** the cost is billable even if no new tree is
  planted (BGH VIII ZR 107/20, Rn. 36–38).
  - **Rule on initial planting not engaged:** in that case there were no
  findings that the tree was part of the garden's initial planting
  (Erstausstattung). Even then, § 2 Nr. 10 BetrKV does not exclude removal costs
  of that kind (BGH VIII ZR 107/20, Rn. 39–40).
  - **Gap: one-off vs. recurring.** The statutes define service charges as costs
  that arise on an ongoing basis (laufend entstehen) (§ 556 BGB; § 1 BetrKV).
  No retrieved passage explains how a felling that happens only once, or every
  few years, meets that test. The Leitsatz only says "grundsätzlich" (in
  principle). The corpus as retrieved does not cover this point. Also unclear
  from the results: whether the cost may be billed all in the year it was
  incurred or must be spread over several years. Refer to a tenancy lawyer.

  **2. Replacing playground sand (Spielsand-Erneuerung): not covered by the
  results**
  - Two separate searches returned no passage that mentions playgrounds
  (Spielplätze) or renewing sand (Erneuerung von Sand). Neither returned the
  text of § 2 BetrKV.
  - I therefore cannot confirm from the corpus whether § 2 Nr. 10 BetrKV covers
  this. Refer to a tenancy lawyer.
  - The only general rule retrieved: a cost must arise on an ongoing basis
  (laufend entstehen) (§ 1 BetrKV). It must also not be maintenance or repair
  (Instandhaltungs- und Instandsetzungskosten) (§ 1 BetrKV). How often the sand
  is replaced is therefore relevant, but no retrieved passage applies these
  rules to sand.

  **3. General requirements (both items)**
  - Tenants only bear service charges (Betriebskosten) if the parties agree on
  it. Service charges are costs that arise on an ongoing basis (laufend
  entstehen) through ownership or the intended use of the property (§ 556 BGB).
  - In a residential lease, a clause (even a standard-form one) saying the
  tenant bears "the service charges" (die Betriebskosten) is enough. It covers
  the costs defined in § 556 Abs. 1 Satz 2 BGB and explained in the BetrKV, even
  without the catalogue attached (BGH VIII ZR 137/15, Leitsatz, decided
  2016-02-10).
  - The bill must be prepared annually and follow the principle of economy
  (Grundsatz der Wirtschaftlichkeit). It must reach the tenant within 12 months
  after the end of the billing period, or any back-payment claim is lost (§ 556
  BGB).

  **Facts the answer depends on**
  - **Lease clause:** whether the lease passes on the service charges
  (Betriebskosten), generally or specifically garden maintenance costs
  (Gartenpflege). Never assume it does.
  - **Tree:** whether it was actually rotten or diseased and unstable, as in BGH
  VIII ZR 107/20. Also whether it was part of the initial planting
  (Erstausstattung), which the court left open on the facts.
  - **Sand:** how often it is replaced (ongoing vs. one-off), and whether the
  playground belongs to the property and the tenants can use it. The corpus does
  not say how either fact affects whether the sand can be billed.
  - **Timing:** that both costs go into a statement delivered on time (§ 556
  BGB).
