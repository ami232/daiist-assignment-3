## Final answer

Yes, under limited conditions. Without consent, email marketing is not allowed: it "may be allowed only in respect of subscribers or users who have given their prior consent" [ePrivacy, Article 13]. There is one exception for existing customers. If you got a customer's email address "in the context of the sale of a product or a service", you may use it for direct marketing of "its own similar products or services". This applies only if customers "clearly and distinctly are given the opportunity to object, free of charge and in an easy manner", both "at the time of their collection and on the occasion of each message" [ePrivacy, Article 13]. You may not hide the sender's identity, and every email needs "a valid address" where the recipient can ask you to stop [ePrivacy, Article 13].

Under the GDPR, customers can object to direct marketing "at any time". After they object, their data "shall no longer be processed for such purposes" [GDPR, Article 21]. You must tell them about this right "at the latest at the time of the first communication" [GDPR, Article 21].

The corpus does not say how each Member State has written these rules into its national law.
