## q07 (multi)

**Question:** What is the difference between an alert limit, an intervention limit and an immediate action limit for track geometry?

**Reference answer:** Alert limit: if exceeded, the track geometry condition must be analysed and considered in regularly planned maintenance. Intervention limit: if exceeded, corrective maintenance is required so the immediate action limit is not reached before the next inspection. Immediate action limit: if exceeded, measures must be taken to reduce the risk of derailment to an acceptable level.

**Agent answer:**

According to Regulation (EU) No 1299/2014 (TSI INF), the three track geometry limits are defined by the level of maintenance or operational response required when exceeded:

* **Alert limit:** The value which, if exceeded, requires that the track geometry condition is analysed and considered in the regularly planned maintenance operations [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, Appendix S (part 1/5)].
* **Intervention limit:** The value which, if exceeded, requires corrective maintenance in order that the immediate action limit shall not be reached before the next inspection [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, Appendix S (part 3/5)].
* **Immediate action limit:** The value which, if exceeded, requires taking measures to reduce the risk of derailment to an acceptable level [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, Appendix S (part 3/5)].

## q10 (single)

**Question:** What longitudinal slip resistance and fatigue performance must a rail fastening system achieve in laboratory tests?

**Reference answer:** At least 7 kN longitudinal force to start rail slip through a single fastening assembly (at least 9 kN for speeds above 250 km/h). It must resist 3 000 000 cycles of the typical sharp-curve load with clamping force and longitudinal restraint degraded by no more than 20 % and vertical stiffness by no more than 25 %.

**Agent answer:**

Under laboratory test conditions, a rail fastening system must meet the following requirements:

* **Longitudinal slip resistance:** The longitudinal force required to cause the rail to begin to slip (i.e. move in an inelastic way) through a single rail fastening assembly must be at least 7 kN, and at least 9 kN for line speeds exceeding 250 km/h [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 5.3.2].
* **Fatigue performance:** The rail fastening system must resist the application of 3,000,000 cycles of the typical load applied in a sharp curve [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 5.3.2]. Following this test:
  * Its performance in terms of clamping force and longitudinal restraint must not degrade by more than 20% [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 5.3.2].
  * Its vertical stiffness must not degrade by more than 25% [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 5.3.2].
  * The typical test load must be appropriate to the maximum axle load the fastening system is designed to accommodate, as well as the specific combination of rail, rail inclination, rail pad, and sleeper type used with it [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 5.3.2].

**Question:** When can a track design be treated as an 'existing track design' to give presumption of conformity for track resistance?

**Reference answer:** When it has been in normal operation for at least one year and the total tonnage over the track was at least 20 million gross tons during that period.

**Agent answer:**

To demonstrate conformity for track resistance (plain line) by reference to an existing track design, the design must meet the operating conditions intended for the subsystem concerned [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 6.2.5.1(1)].

1. The track design has been in normal operation for at least one year [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 6.2.5.1(3)(a)]; and

To confirm an existing track design, it must be checked that its technical characteristics (Appendix C.1 to the TSI) and operating conditions applied in normal operation (Appendix D.1 to the TSI) are specified, and that reference to the previous use of the track design is available [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 6.2.5.1(2), (4), (5)].

## q13 (multi)

**Question:** If we replace worn rails like-for-like during routine maintenance, do we need an EC verification and an authorisation for placing in service?

**Reference answer:** No. Substitution in the framework of maintenance (replacement by parts of identical function and performance during preventive or corrective maintenance) does not require formal verification or authorisation. Replacements should still follow the TSI as far as reasonably practicable, and line layout, track parameters, S&C, track resistance, structures and platforms should be adapted together to move towards interoperability.

**Agent answer:**

**No**, you do not need an EC verification or an authorisation for placing in service when replacing worn rails like-for-like during routine maintenance.

* **No authorisation or EC verification for maintenance:** Where parts of an infrastructure subsystem are maintained, formal verification and authorisation for placing into service are not required `[Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 7.3.3]`. Directive (EU) 2016/797 categorises replacing components with parts of identical function and performance in the framework of preventive or corrective maintenance as "substitution in the framework of maintenance" `[Dir_2016_797, Article 2(13)-(22)]`. Authorisation for placing in service and EC verification procedures apply to new subsystems or to the renewal and upgrading of existing subsystems, not routine maintenance `[Dir_2016_797, Article 15(1)-(5)]`, `[Dir_2016_797, Article 18(1)-(4)]`, `[Dir_2016_797, Article 18(5)-(6)]`.
* **Maintenance responsibilities:** Even though formal verification and authorisation are not required, maintenance replacements should, as far as reasonably practicable, be undertaken in accordance with the TSI INF requirements `[Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 7.3.3]`. The body responsible for maintenance must ensure that replacement components are suitable for their applications, used within their area of use, traceable, and meet essential requirements `[Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 6.5.3]`, `[Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 6.6.3]`.

## q14 (multi)

**Question:** When is the infrastructure subsystem of a line considered 'upgraded' rather than renewed under the TSI INF?

**Reference answer:** When at least the performance parameters axle load or gauge are changed to meet the requirements of another traffic code. Renewal is systematic replacement of elements without changing performance parameters.

**Agent answer:**

Under the TSI INF, the infrastructure subsystem of a line is distinguished between upgrading and renewal as follows:

* **Upgrading** means any major modification work on a subsystem or part of a subsystem which improves the overall performance of the subsystem [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 7.3.1]. In the specific context of the TSI INF, the infrastructure subsystem of a line is considered to be upgraded when at least the performance parameters **axle load** or **gauge** are changed in order to meet the requirements of another traffic code [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 7.3.1].
* **Renewal** means any major substitution work on a subsystem or part of a subsystem which does not change the overall performance of the subsystem [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 7.3.2]. Renewal is the same case as upgrading, but without a change in performance parameters [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 7.3.2].

## q17 (single)

**Question:** Ride instability has been reported on a section where trains run at 200 km/h. What does the infrastructure manager measure and what equivalent conicity limit applies?

**Reference answer:** The IM localises the section in a joint investigation with the railway undertaking, measures track gauge and railhead profiles at about every 10 m, and calculates the mean equivalent conicity over 100 m. For 160 < v <= 230 km/h the limit is 0,30. If the limit is met, a joint investigation determines the reason for the instability.

**Agent answer:**

When ride instability is reported, the infrastructure manager must undertake the following steps [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 4.2.11.2(1)]:

* **What to measure:** The infrastructure manager shall measure the **track gauge** and the **railhead profiles** at the site in question at intervals of approximately 10 m [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 4.2.11.2(2)].
* **Calculation:** The mean equivalent conicity over 100 m is calculated by modelling with the specified reference wheelsets [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 4.2.11.2(2)].
* **Limit value:** For a section operating at 200 km/h (which falls in the speed range 160 < v ≤ 230 km/h), the maximum limit value for the mean equivalent conicity over 100 m is **0.30** [Reg_1299_2014, Annex, 4.2.11.2(2)].

## q20 (multi)

**Question:** Which track-related precursors to accidents must be reported as common safety indicators, and how is a 'broken rail' defined?

**Reference answer:** Precursors include broken rail and track buckle or other track misalignment (plus wrong-side signalling failure, SPADs, broken wheels and axles). A broken rail is a rail separated into two or more pieces, or one from which a piece of metal detaches causing a gap more than 50 mm long and more than 10 mm deep on the running surface.

**Agent answer:**

The track-related precursors to accidents that must be reported as common safety indicators (CSIs) are:
* **Broken rail** [Dir_2016_798, Annex I, 4]

## q28 (multi)

**Question:** What are the four maintenance functions of an entity in charge of maintenance, which may be outsourced, and how long is an ECM certificate valid?

**Reference answer:** Management, maintenance development, fleet-maintenance management and maintenance delivery.
