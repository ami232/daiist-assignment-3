## q01 (single)

**Question:** We found part of a Roman harbour still buried on Spain's continental shelf. Can it count as Spanish historical heritage while it's still down there?

**Reference answer:** Yes. Historic remains on the continental shelf that can be studied using archaeological methods can qualify even if nobody has brought them up.

**Expected:** Ley 16/1985 Artículo 40

**Assistant's answer:**

> Yes, the harbor counts as Spanish historical heritage even while it remains on the continental shelf, regardless of whether it has been extracted [Ley 16/1985, Artículo 40]. 
> 
> The law specifies that movable or immovable property of historical character capable of being studied archaeologically forms part of the Spanish Historical Heritage, «hayan sido o no extraídos y tanto si se encuentran en la superficie o en el subsuelo, en el mar territorial o en la plataforma continental» (whether they have been extracted or not and whether they are found on the surface or in the subsoil, in the territorial sea or on the continental shelf) [Ley 16/1985, Artículo 40].

## q03 (single)

**Question:** I spotted a Roman coin by chance on a dive in Spain. Who owns it, and can I wait until the end of the month to report it?

**Reference answer:** The coin is public property. Since you found it by chance, you have to report it to the relevant authorities immediately.

**Expected:** Ley 16/1985 Artículo 44

**Assistant's answer:**

> All archaeological items discovered by chance are public property [Ley 16/1985, Artículo 44], meaning you do not own the coin. Failing to report it immediately strips you of any potential finders' reward and exposes you to legal penalties [Ley 16/1985, Artículo 44].
> 
> This is information from the legal texts, not legal advice. Report any find to the authorities before acting.

## q12 (single)

**Question:** An abandoned landing stage with archaeological value has been partly underwater at high tide for exactly 100 years. Can it count as underwater cultural heritage even though it's exposed at low tide?

**Reference answer:** Yes. Exactly 100 years meets the minimum, and the rules count traces of human activity that have been underwater partly or periodically.

**Expected:** UNESCO 2001 Artículo 1

**Assistant's answer:**

> Yes, it can count as underwater cultural heritage because the definition includes traces of human existence that have been under water periodically or continuously for at least 100 years.
> 
> As stated in [UNESCO 2001, Artículo 1]: «por «patrimonio cultural subacuático» se entiende todos los rastros de existencia humana que tengan un carácter cultural, histórico o arqueológico, que hayan estado bajo el agua, parcial o totalmente, de forma periódica o continua, por lo menos durante 100 años», which means "underwater cultural heritage means all traces of human existence having a cultural, historical or archaeological character which have been partially or totally under water, periodically or continuously, for at least 100 years."
> 
> This is information from the legal texts, not legal advice. Report any find to the authorities before acting.

> (No activity relating to underwater cultural heritage to which this Convention applies shall be subject to the law of salvage and finders, unless it: a) is authorized by the competent authorities, and b) is in full conformity with this Convention, and c) ensures that any recovery of the underwater cultural heritage is achieved to its maximum protection.)

**Reference answer:** You can charge for authorised professional archaeological services that fully comply with the convention. The museum can't pay you in wreck coins to sell because the finds can't be traded as commercial goods.

**Expected:** UNESCO 2001 Norma 2

**Assistant's answer:**

> Yes, you can charge for professional archaeological services if they are duly authorized, as the rules do not prohibit «la prestación de servicios arqueológicos profesionales o de servicios conexos necesarios cuya índole y finalidad sean plenamente conformes con la presente Convención» (the provision of professional archaeological services or necessary allied services whose nature and purpose are fully in conformity with this Convention) [UNESCO 2001, Norma 2].
> 
> However, you cannot be paid in wreck coins to sell, because underwater cultural heritage must not be traded, as «el patrimonio cultural subacuático no deberá ser objeto de transacciones ni de operaciones de venta, compra o trueque como bien comercial» (underwater cultural heritage shall not be traded, sold, bought or bartered as commercial goods) [UNESCO 2001, Norma 2].

> This is information from the legal texts, not legal advice. Report any find to the authorities before acting.
