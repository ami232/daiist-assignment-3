## q01 (single)
**Question:** When may an institution suspend the counting of days past due because the obligor disputes the obligation?

**Reference:** Only if a court or other formal external body issues a binding ruling, or, for leasing, a formal complaint is confirmed by independent internal audit or validation (paragraph 19).

**Answer:** An institution may suspend the counting of days past due when a credit obligation is the subject of a dispute between the obligor and the institution until the dispute is resolved, provided that at least one of the following conditions is met:

(a) The dispute over the existence or amount of the credit obligation has been introduced to a court or another formal procedure performed by a dedicated external body that results in a binding ruling [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 22]; or  
(b) In the specific case of leasing, a formal complaint has been directed to the institution about the object of the contract and the merit of the complaint has been confirmed by independent internal audit, internal validation or another comparable independent auditing unit [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 22].

## q06 (single)
**Question:** What is the minimum probation period before a defaulted exposure can return to non-defaulted status?

**Reference:** Not shorter than 3 months from when the obligor was no longer more than 90 days past due and no indication of unlikeliness to pay still applied; institutions may use longer periods.

**Answer:** The minimum probation period should not be shorter than 3 months from the moment the obligor is no longer past due more than 90 days and no indication of unlikeliness to pay applies [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 11-12]. 

For exposures under distressed restructuring, a longer probation period of at least 1 year applies from the latest of the extension of restructuring measures, classification as default, or the end of any grace period, alongside additional conditions such as a material payment [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 12, p. 35-36].

## q09 (single)
**Question:** Under which condition can the specific treatment with a 180-day past-due limit be applied?

**Reference:** The obligation is past due not longer than 180 days and there are no other indications of unlikeliness to pay (paragraphs 25-26).

**Answer:** Institutions may apply the specific treatment with a 180-day past-due limit to exposures to central governments, local authorities, and public sector entities when all of the following conditions are met:
* The contract relates to the supply of goods or services, where administrative procedures require specific controls before payment can be made (excluding instruments like bonds) [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 23].
* Apart from the payment delay, there are no other indications of unlikeliness to pay, the obligor’s financial situation is sound, and there are no reasonable concerns that the obligation will not be paid in full [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 23].
* The obligation is past due by no longer than 180 days [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 23].

## q11 (single)
**Question:** What should an institution do if the historical observation period contains no or too few bad years when estimating the long-run average default rate?

**Reference:** Adjust the average of observed one-year default rates to estimate the long-run average (paragraph 85(a)).

**Answer:** Where the historical observation period contains no or insufficient bad years, institutions should adjust the average of observed one-year default rates to estimate a long-run average default rate [EBA-GL-2017-16, p. 69]. 

Institutions must ensure that this adjustment results in a long-run average default rate that reflects the likely range of variability of default rates [EBA-GL-2017-16, p. 69].

## q29 (multi)
**Question:** When does the days-past-due count start for factoring, and how is an obligor defaulting more than once treated in the one-year default rate?

**Reference:** Factoring: when the account is in debit (definition of default guidelines). One-year default rate: the obligor is counted once in numerator and denominator (PD/LGD guidelines).

**Answer:** For factoring arrangements, the counting of days past due depends on how the factor recognises the exposures [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 24-25]:
* **Direct exposures to debtors (purchased receivables):** Counting commences when the payment for a single receivable becomes due [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 24].
* **Exposures where ceded receivables are not on the balance sheet and the factor is liable up to an agreed percentage:** Counting commences when the factoring account is in debit (i.e., when advances paid for receivables exceed the agreed percentage) [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 24]. 

For the one-year default rate calculation, each defaulted obligor must be **counted only once** in both the numerator and the denominator, even if that obligor defaults more than once during the relevant one-year observation period [EBA-GL-2017-16, p. 67-68].

**Question:** What probation period applies before return to non-default, and what threshold decides default after a distressed restructuring?

**Reference:** Probation of at least 3 months; distressed restructuring defaulted if diminished financial obligation exceeds the 1% threshold.

**Answer:** A probation period of **at least 1 year** applies before returning to non-defaulted status following a distressed restructuring [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 12]. This period runs from the latest of:
1. The moment of extending the restructuring measures [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 12, p. 35];
2. The moment when the exposure was classified as defaulted [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 12, p. 35]; or
3. The end of any grace period included in the restructuring arrangements [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 12, p. 35].

Additionally, this probation period cannot be shorter than the period during which a material payment has been made by the obligor (equal to the previously past-due amount or written-off amount under forbearance measures) [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 12].

A distressed restructuring is classified as a default if the diminished financial obligation (calculated as the percentage drop in net present value of expected cash flows discounted at the original effective interest rate before and after restructuring) **exceeds a threshold set by the institution that cannot be higher than 1%** [EBA-GL-2016-07, p. 29].
