## q01  [single]
**Q:** What is the recommended dose of apixaban for the prevention of stroke in adults with non-valvular atrial fibrillation?

**Reference:** 5 mg taken orally twice daily.

**Answer:**
The recommended dose of apixaban for the prevention of stroke and systemic embolism in adult patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation is 5 mg taken orally twice daily [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, pp. 2-3]. 

A reduced dose of 2.5 mg taken orally twice daily is recommended for patients with at least two of the following characteristics: age ≥ 80 years, body weight ≤ 60 kg, or serum creatinine ≥ 1.5 mg/dL (133 micromole/L) [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, pp. 2-3].

## q06  [single]
**Q:** Is apixaban recommended in patients taking strong inhibitors of both CYP3A4 and P-gp such as ketoconazole or ritonavir?

**Q:** By how much did activated charcoal given 2 hours after ingestion reduce apixaban exposure?

**Q:** How much did immediate-release verapamil taken one hour before dabigatran increase dabigatran exposure?

## q22  [multi]
**Q:** What do the apixaban and dabigatran SmPCs say about taking ketoconazole at the same time?

**Reference:** Apixaban: use not recommended with strong inhibitors of both CYP3A4 and P-gp such as ketoconazole. Dabigatran: concomitant systemic ketoconazole is contraindicated.

**Answer:**
The apixaban and dabigatran SmPCs state the following regarding concomitant use with ketoconazole:

* **Apixaban:** Co-administration with ketoconazole (a strong inhibitor of both CYP3A4 and P-gp) leads to a 2-fold increase in mean apixaban AUC and a 1.6-fold increase in mean Cmax [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, pp. 11-12]. The use of apixaban is not recommended in patients receiving concomitant systemic treatment with strong inhibitors of both CYP3A4 and P-gp, such as azole-antimycotics (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, and posaconazole) [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, pp. 11-12].
* **Dabigatran:** Ketoconazole is listed as a P-gp inhibitor whose concomitant use with dabigatran etexilate is **contraindicated** [pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, pp. 11-12]. Ketoconazole increases total dabigatran AUC and Cmax values by approximately 2.4-fold to 2.5-fold after single or multiple oral doses [pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, pp. 11-12].

**Reference:** For both, stop the VKA and start the anticoagulant when the INR is below 2 (apixaban: INR < 2; dabigatran: INR < 2.0).

**Answer:**
When switching treatment from a vitamin K antagonist (VKA) to apixaban or dabigatran:

* **To Apixaban:** Discontinue the warfarin or other VKA therapy and start apixaban when the international normalised ratio (INR) is < 2 [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, pp. 4-5].
* **To Dabigatran:** Stop the VKA, and dabigatran etexilate can be given as soon as the INR is < 2.0 [pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, p. 6].

## q24  [multi]
**Q:** What do the apixaban and dabigatran SmPCs say about using another anticoagulant at the same time?

**Reference:** Concomitant treatment with any other anticoagulant is contraindicated in both, except when switching anticoagulant therapy, when UFH is given to keep a central venous or arterial catheter open, or during catheter ablation for atrial fibrillation.

**Answer:**
Both the apixaban and dabigatran SmPCs state that concomitant treatment with any other anticoagulants is contraindicated, due to an increased risk of bleeding [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, p. 6; pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, p. 6]. 

Exceptions to this contraindication for both agents are:
* Specific circumstances when switching anticoagulant therapy [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, p. 6; pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, p. 6].
* When unfractionated heparin (UFH) is given at doses necessary to maintain an open central venous or arterial catheter [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, p. 6; pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, p. 6].
* When UFH is given during catheter ablation for atrial fibrillation [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, p. 6; pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, p. 6].

## q25  [multi]
**Q:** How do the apixaban and dabigatran SmPCs differ on patients with prosthetic heart valves?

**Reference:** Apixaban is not recommended (not studied in prosthetic valves). Dabigatran is contraindicated in prosthetic heart valves requiring anticoagulant treatment.

**Answer:**
The apixaban and dabigatran SmPCs differ in how they classify the use of the drug in patients with prosthetic heart valves:

* **Dabigatran (Pradaxa):** Prosthetic heart valves requiring anticoagulant treatment are explicitly listed as a **contraindication** (Section 4.3) [pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, p. 6 (section 4.3)]. Furthermore, clinical trials in patients with prosthetic heart valves showed higher rates of thromboembolic and bleeding events (particularly haemorrhagic pericardial effusions in early post-operative patients) compared to warfarin (Section 5.1) [pradaxa-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 3, p. 23 (section 5.1)].
* **Apixaban (Eliquis):** The safety and efficacy of apixaban have not been studied in patients with prosthetic heart valves, and therefore its use is **not recommended** in this setting rather than strictly contraindicated (Section 4.4) [eliquis-epar-product-information_en | SmPC 1 of 4, pp. 7-8 (section 4.4)].
