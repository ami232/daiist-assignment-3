  **Answer (English, from the corpus only)**

  **Legal deposit (fianza).** When the contract is signed, a cash deposit is
  mandatory: **one month's rent for a housing lease** (arrendamiento de
  vivienda) and **two months' rent for other uses** (uso distinto del de
  vivienda) [LAU, Artículo 36].

  **Additional guarantee (garantía adicional).** Landlord and tenant may agree
  on an extra guarantee on top of the cash deposit [LAU, Artículo 36]. Its
  limits:
  - For a housing lease, in contracts **of up to five years, or up to seven
  years if the landlord is a legal entity (persona jurídica)**, the extra
  guarantee **"no podrá exceder de dos mensualidades de renta"** (may not be
  more than two months' rent) [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - For **temporary leases** (arrendamientos temporales) it may not be more than
  **one month's rent** [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - The tenant can never be required to take out rent-default insurance
  ("seguros de impago de renta u otras coberturas análogas") [LAU, Artículo 36].

  So for a normal flat lease of up to five or seven years, the landlord can ask
  for at most 1 month of deposit plus up to 2 months of additional guarantee.
  The law also says the landlord can never require more than one month's rent
  paid in advance [LAU, Artículo 17].

  **Updating the deposit.**
  - The deposit **cannot be updated during the first five years, or the first
  seven years if the landlord is a legal entity**. At each extension (prórroga),
  the landlord can ask to raise it, or the tenant to lower it, to match one or
  two months of the current rent, as applicable [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - If the agreed term is longer than five years (seven for a legal entity),
  updates after that point follow what the contract says. If the contract says
  nothing, the rent-update clause is presumed to apply to the deposit too [LAU,
  Artículo 36].

  **Return.**
  - Whatever balance of the deposit is owed back to the tenant earns **statutory
  interest (interés legal)** if it has not been returned **one month after the
  tenant hands back the keys** [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - When the contract ends, both parties must sign a written end-of-lease
  document recording the flat's condition. If no document is signed, or it
  records no damage, the flat is presumed to have been returned in good
  condition unless shown otherwise [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - Public administrations (and similar bodies listed in the law) do not have to
  pay a deposit when the rent is paid from their budgets [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - Each autonomous community may require landlords to deposit the fianza with a
  regional body, without interest, until the contract ends. If that body does
  not return it within one month of the end of the contract, it earns statutory
  interest [LAU, Disposición adicional tercera].

  **Gaps and caveats.**
  - My searches found no Ley 12/2023 passage that amends Article 36. I can't
  confirm or rule out that Ley 12/2023 changed it, so I cite only the LAU.
  - The corpus doesn't say whether each region actually requires the deposit to
  be lodged, or how. Those are regional rules and are not in the indexed
  documents.
  - The corpus doesn't give a fixed number of days by which the landlord must
  return the deposit. It only sets the interest rule after one month.

  This is information from the laws, not legal advice, and the consolidated text
  has no legal value on its own.

⏺ For a normal flat rental, your landlord must take one month's rent as a cash
  deposit (fianza) when you sign [LAU, Artículo 36]. Two months applies only to
  leases that aren't for housing [LAU, Artículo 36].

  On top of that, the landlord can also ask for an additional guarantee, but
  there are limits:
  - Up to two months' rent on a housing lease of up to 5 years, or up to 7 years
    if the landlord is a company (persona jurídica) [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - Up to one month's rent on a temporary lease [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - You can never be required to take out rent-default insurance ("seguros de
    impago de renta") [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - The landlord can never require more than one month's rent paid in advance
    [LAU, Artículo 17].

  So on a normal lease the most you can be asked for is 1 month of deposit plus 
  up to 2 months of additional guarantee, and at most one month of rent in
  advance.

  Other points:
  - Updating the deposit: it can't be changed during the first 5 years (7 if the
    landlord is a company). At each extension after that, it can be raised or
    lowered to match the current rent [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - Getting it back: any amount owed to you earns statutory interest (interés
    legal) if it hasn't been returned one month after you hand back the keys
    [LAU, Artículo 36]. The law also expects a signed written record of the
    flat's condition when the lease ends. If none is signed, the flat is
    presumed to have been returned in good condition [LAU, Artículo 36].
  - Regional deposit: each autonomous community can require the landlord to
    lodge the deposit with a regional body [LAU, Disposición adicional tercera].
    The indexed documents don't say what your region requires.
