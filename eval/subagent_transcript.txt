  **Answer: No, not as a rule. The 3% weight passes the asset-spreading test,
  but buying 15% of one issuer's debt securities breaks the 10% cap on how much
  of one issuer's debt a fund may acquire.**

  **Rule**
  - **Debt ownership cap:** "A UCITS may acquire no more than ... (b) 10 % of
  the debt securities of a single issuing body" [ucits_directive, Article
  56(2)]. A 15% holding of the company's bonds is above this cap. The fund's 3%
  weight in that issuer does not change this: the cap is a share of the issuer's
  debt in issue, not a share of the fund's assets.
  - **Asset-spreading limit:** Article 52 refers to "the 5 % limit laid down in
  the first subparagraph of paragraph 1" for transferable securities issued by
  the same body. Member States may raise it "to a maximum of 10 %". If they do,
  the holdings in issuers that each take up more than 5% of assets "shall not
  exceed 40 % of the value of its assets" [ucits_directive, Article 52(2)]. A 3%
  position is below the 5% figure, so Article 52 is not the problem. Two
  caveats:
    - The opening sentence of Article 52(1) did not come back in the search
  results. I only saw the 5% limit through those cross-references.
    - The fund's combined exposure to that issuer still must not go above 20% of
  assets where it combines securities, deposits and OTC derivative exposures
  with that body [ucits_directive, Article 52(2)]. Companies in the same
  consolidated group count as a single body [ucits_directive, Article 52 to
  Article 53].

  **Conditions and exceptions**
  1. **When the cap can be disregarded:** the 10% debt limit "may be disregarded
  at the time of acquisition if at that time the gross amount of the debt
  securities ... or the net amount of the securities in issue, cannot be
  calculated" [ucits_directive, Article 56]. This only covers the moment of
  purchase when the total in issue is unknown. It is not a general permission to
  hold 15%.
  2. **Possible national waiver:** a Member State may waive Article 56(1) and
  (2) for securities issued or guaranteed by a Member State or its local
  authorities, by a third country, or by a public international body to which
  one or more Member States belong [ucits_directive, Article 56(3)]. That does
  not fit ordinary corporate bonds.
  3. **Index-tracking funds:** Article 53 allows higher Article 52 limits for
  these funds only "Without prejudice to the limits laid down in Article 56"
  [ucits_directive, Article 52 to Article 53]. So the 10% debt cap still
  applies.
  4. **Fund level or management-company level:** for debt, the passage says "A
  UCITS may acquire", so the cap is worded at the level of each UCITS
  [ucits_directive, Article 55 to Article 56]. The aggregation across "all of
  the common funds which it manages" in Article 56(1) applies only to voting
  shares that would give significant influence. The passages returned do not say
  whether the debt cap is also added up across sibling funds, so legal should
  confirm that point.

  **Note:** this answer reflects only the original Directive 2009/65/EC text
  that is indexed. Later amendments (e.g. UCITS V) and national law may matter,
  especially for any Member State choice or waiver under Articles 52, 53 and 56.
