## q02 (single)
Q: At what age can a child give valid consent for online services under the GDPR, and can Member States change it?
REF: 16 years by default. Member States may provide by law a lower age, but not below 13. Below that age, consent must be given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility.
EXPECTED: GDPR | Article 8
ANSWER: Under the GDPR, in relation to the offer of information society services directly to a child, the processing of a child's personal data based on consent is lawful where the child is at least 16 years old [GDPR, Article 8]. If the child is below the age of 16 years, the processing is lawful only if and to the extent that consent is given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility over the child [GDPR, Article 8].

Member States may provide by law for a lower age for those purposes, provided that such lower age is not below 13 years [GDPR, Article 8].

## q16 (single)
Q: How must a request for consent be presented if it is in a written declaration that also concerns other matters?
REF: It must be clearly distinguishable from the other matters, in an intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language.
EXPECTED: GDPR | Article 7
ANSWER: If a data subject's consent is given in the context of a written declaration that also concerns other matters, the request for consent must be presented in a manner that is:

* **Clearly distinguishable** from the other matters [GDPR, Article 7];
* In an **intelligible and easily accessible form** [GDPR, Article 7]; and
* Using **clear and plain language** [GDPR, Article 7].

Additionally, any part of such a declaration that constitutes an infringement of the GDPR is not binding [GDPR, Article 7].

## q20 (single)
Q: What is bundling of consent and why is it a problem?
REF: Bundling means tying consent to the acceptance of terms or to a service when the processing is not necessary for it. Under Article 7(4) GDPR this is presumed not to be freely given, so the consent is probably invalid.
EXPECTED: EDPB-05-2020-consent | Section 3.1.2

It also refers to situations where several processing operations or purposes are conflated into a non-negotiable package without allowing separate consent for each individual operation [EDPB-05-2020-consent, p. 10, 12].

## q25 (single)

REF: It must notify the controller without undue delay. The controller stays responsible for notifying the supervisory authority.
EXPECTED: EDPB-09-2022-breach-notification | p. 13
ANSWER: When a processor discovers a personal data breach concerning personal data it is processing on behalf of a controller, it must:

* **Notify the controller without undue delay** after becoming aware of the personal data breach [GDPR, Article 33]. The processor does not need to first assess the likelihood of risk arising from the breach before making this notification [EDPB-09-2022-breach-notification, p. 13].
* **Assist the controller** in ensuring compliance with obligations regarding security, breach notifications, and data protection impact assessments, taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to the processor [EDPB-09-2022-breach-notification, p. 13].
